Anonymization / contamination check
====================================================================
pairs re-scored          : 75 of 75
entities masked          : 702 total, 9.4 per pair
distinct companies       : 40
wall clock               : 0.0 min

Masking replaces company names, tickers, executive and analyst names,
products, and places with placeholders, then re-scores with the
identical judge (same prompt, model, temperature).

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. STABILITY -- masked vs. original judge scores
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  Pearson r                    : +0.861  (p = 3.86e-23)
  quadratic-weighted kappa     : 0.730
  unweighted kappa             : 0.652
  mean change (masked - orig)  : +1.83 points on 0-100
  mean |change|                : 4.33
  identical scores             : 41/75
  primary_type agreement       : 61/75

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. VALIDATION SURVIVAL -- does agreement with humans hold up?
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  Reference: the two human annotators agree with each other at r = 0.587.
  That is the benchmark any judge should be measured against.

comparison                            original      masked    change
  Pearson r, vs human1                  0.777       0.582    -0.195
  Pearson r, vs human2                  0.803       0.771    -0.032
  Pearson r, vs human mean              0.886       0.773    -0.113

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. CONFOUND -- masking also removes genuine specificity
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  mean score moved +1.83 points; 24/75 pairs moved UP
  masked mean 28.92 vs original 27.08

  The shift is upward -- masked responses read as MORE evasive. That is
  what you expect mechanically: replacing named products, places, and
  companies with placeholders strips concrete detail out of the text,
  and 'vagueness' is one of the four scored dimensions. So this check
  cannot cleanly separate two explanations for any drop:
    (a) the judge lost access to memorised company identity, or
    (b) masking destroyed real specificity that the judge -- and the
        human annotators -- were both legitimately reading.
  The confound biases the test TOWARD finding an effect, which makes a
  stable result conservative and a small drop hard to attribute.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
VERDICT
--------------------------------------------------------------------
  Contamination is NOT driving the results.
    - masked vs original scores correlate at r = 0.861, with 41/75 scores unchanged
    - the mean score moves only +1.83 points on a 0-100 scale
    - after masking, the judge still agrees with the human mean at
      r = 0.773, well above the 0.587 the two
      annotators achieve with each other
  Agreement does fall, and the paper should report that plainly rather
  than claim the check was passed without cost -- but the residual is
  as consistent with the specificity confound above as with recall.
